## 5. Final Essay Version B — "The Judgment Layer" (Analytical Architecture Edition)

In 2025, a multicenter trial withdrew AI assistance from endoscopists who had come to rely on it. Their adenoma detection rate on unassisted procedures fell from 28.4% to 22.4%. The trial's authors recommended mandatory non-AI practice to retain the skill. Perception had degraded because the AI had made perceiving unnecessary.

The trial measured a perceptual skill. Organizations run on deliberative judgment, which has a different learning structure but the same dependency. Judgment is built by making consequential decisions and living with their consequences. When an AI absorbs the consequence-bearing step, the human ratifies an outcome they could not have produced, and the capability that made the role worth having decays. The organization keeps the headcount, keeps the outputs, and loses the judgment.

Most organizations are walking into this trap. They automate coordination, keep humans "in the loop," and assume that presence equals judgment. A human approving a recommendation is a compliance control; active deliberation is a capability. Controls are cheap, whereas capabilities require load.

### Two Reasons to Protect a Node

A judgment node is worth protecting for two distinct reasons, and conflating them is the most common design error:
1. **Decision Value:** The human's judgment improves *this* decision now.
2. **Capability Value:** The human's judgment here builds the capacity to make better decisions *later*.

A junior researcher's first ten prioritization calls may not beat the AI's default. If those calls are automated away, the researcher never reaches the eleventh call, where they would. Protecting only decision value produces an organization that is efficient today and hollow in five years. Protecting only capability value produces an organization that pays for training it never uses.
